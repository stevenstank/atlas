# Phase 3 baseline: 2026-10-20, Ryzen 7 7435HS under WSL2

**This is the Phase 3 baseline campaign** of the unoptimized Atlas engine at
commit `cf8f5c2`, measured with benchmark suite version 1
([../BENCHMARKS.md](../BENCHMARKS.md)) under decision D-007 as amended on
2026-10-14 ([../DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md)). It is the one campaign that
amendment authorized, and it is the authoritative record of that campaign.
It records measurements only. The bottleneck analysis remains
[2026-10-06-ryzen7-7435hs-wsl2-analysis.md](2026-10-06-ryzen7-7435hs-wsl2-analysis.md);
no profiles were taken in this campaign. The 2026-10-06 report and its
analysis are unchanged and keep their own judgement under the original
D-007 decision.

**Result.** Under the prospective D-007 rule, no wall-time (`sec/op`)
comparison between set A and set B is significant after Holm–Bonferroni
correction at family-wise α = 0.05 (section 5). That is "no significant
difference detected". It is **not** evidence that the sets are equivalent.
Set B was slower than set A in 18 of 21 workloads, by a geometric mean of
6.6% (section 9).

## 3. Design and artifact integrity

Two sets, A then B, of 10 rounds; each round runs all 21 workloads once, one
`atlasbench -bench NAME -format bench` process per workload, profiles off,
in an order shuffled for that round from the master seed (BENCHMARKS.md §5).
That is 2 × 10 × 21 = 420 invocations. There was no warm-up run.

Checked after the campaign, from the artifacts:

- `status` is `complete`; there is no `failures.txt`.
- `manifest.tsv` has 420 records, 210 per set, in exactly the order of
  `plan.tsv`, every one with exit status 0 and a numeric wall time.
- `plan.tsv` is reproduced byte for byte by
  `atlasbench -plan -seed 10739372551684200573 -rounds 10`, and
  `workloads.txt` equals the suite table.
- `A.txt` and `B.txt` are each exactly the concatenation of their planned
  invocations' stdout logs. Each holds 10 results for each of the 21
  workloads, none malformed. All 420 stderr logs are empty.
- Every manifest wall time equals the `ns/op` of its result line.
- The driver binary's SHA-256 still equals the one in `campaign.txt`.

## 5. Wall-time comparison and the D-007 criterion

The rule (D-007 amendment, BENCHMARKS.md §5) was fixed before the data: the
sets agree only if no `sec/op` comparison is significant after Holm
correction across all 21 at family-wise α = 0.05. The second `benchstat`
run uses α = 0.05/21 = 0.002380952380952381, the first Holm threshold.
**All 21 `sec/op` rows show `~` in that run** (section 6), so Holm rejects
nothing and the criterion is met.

The table orders the comparisons by p-value, as Holm does. Medians are
`benchstat`'s centers. "B vs A" is (B − A) / A of the medians. p is the
exact two-sided Mann–Whitney U p-value, computed from `A.txt` and `B.txt`
(there are no tied values); it equals `benchstat`'s printed `p=` after
rounding, in every row. The Holm-adjusted p is
maxⱼ≤ᵢ min(1, (22 − j) · p₍ⱼ₎). The step-down stops at rank 1, where
0.00288 > 0.00238.

**What this shows and does not show.** No comparison survives the
correction, so no difference between the sets was detected under the rule.
As D-007 states, this does not establish statistical equivalence: with 10
runs per set, the correction lowers the power to detect a real difference.
The margin is small (the smallest p is 1.2 times its threshold), and seven
comparisons are below 0.05 before correction, all with B slower
(section 9).

## 7. Peak RSS, allocations, and GC

- **Peak RSS** (`peak-rss-bytes`, resident memory including the runtime's
  ~4 MiB baseline; see the 2026-10-06 report, section 6): no comparison is
  significant; geometric mean −0.09%.
- **B/op and allocs/op** medians agree between the sets to within 0.02% in
  every workload. Only B8/pad=64/k=10 is flagged (p = 0.020 and 0.043), for
  a difference of about one allocation. Allocation counts can vary slightly
  between processes; the semantic check fixes the result, not the runtime's
  allocation pattern.
- **GC.** GC cycles show no difference. GC CPU fraction differs only for
  B8/pad=64/k=40 (+3.1%, p = 0.041); it is unavailable for workloads with no

## 9. Set B slowdown and session drift

Set B was slower than set A in 18 of 21 workloads (geometric mean of the
medians +6.57%), and every comparison with uncorrected p < 0.05 has B
slower. The work was identical (section 7), so the difference is in the
conditions of the session, not in what was computed.

**When it started.** Each run's wall time divided by the median of its
workload over all 20 runs, then the median of those ratios per round:

| Set | R1 | R2 | R3 | R4 | R5 | R6 | R7 | R8 | R9 | R10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 0.897 | 0.963 | 0.962 | 1.015 | 0.937 | 0.942 | 0.925 | 0.950 | 1.000 | 1.022 |
| B | 1.072 | 1.010 | 1.005 | 1.027 | 0.994 | 1.006 | 1.003 | 1.022 | 1.008 | 1.001 |

Rounds 1–8 of set A ran faster. From about set A round 9 (16:17:03Z), the
level rose and stayed there through set B. The largest workloads show it
most clearly; for example, B2/k=40 was 0.86–0.99 of its median in A rounds
1–8 and 0.99–1.17 in A rounds 9–10 and all of set B. So the change looks
like a shift in the machine's state partway through the session, and it
falls almost entirely on set B. **It is confounded with set order**: set B
always runs after set A (BENCHMARKS.md §5, "Limitation"), so this campaign
cannot separate a set effect from drift over time.

**What it is not.** Position within a round has no visible effect: the
median ratio by position ranges from 0.966 to 1.031 with no trend. The
randomized order therefore did what it was meant to do, and the
2026-10-06 confound of fixed position and predecessor is absent.

**Load.** `loadavg.tsv` (1-minute load) rose from 0.09 before set A to
about 1.1 by the end of set A, as expected from one continuously runnable
benchmark process. It reached 2.12 at the start of set B round 7, but that
round's timings are typical (ratio 1.003). The cause of the shift is not
observable from the WSL2 guest (section 11).

These figures do not support a claim that the engine became faster or
slower, because:

- **Provenance.** The 2026-10-06 tree was dirty (`atlas-dirty: true`,
  `fb4bbdb` plus uncommitted changes); its report states the engine and
  models were unchanged, but the exact driver source is not recoverable.
  The driver changed after it (`2367ceb`, `424bb7c`).
- **Order.** 2026-10-06 ran set A in suite order and set B in reverse, so
  each workload had a fixed position and predecessor per set. This campaign
  shuffled every round.
- **Runtime.** `GODEBUG` was not recorded on 2026-10-06. Its `GOGC`,
  `GOMEMLIMIT`, and `GOMAXPROCS` were recorded as default, default, and 16.
  In this campaign the four variables were unset by the script.
- **Machine state.** Host load, power, frequency, and thermal state were
  not observable in either session (section 11), and section 9 shows that
  they can move results by about 10% within one session.

## 11. Run hygiene

- Observable: 1/5/15-minute load average before the sets `0.09 0.19 0.13`,
  after `1.39 0.97 0.49`. Free memory before the sets was not recorded.
  The largest workload peaked near 1.44 GiB RSS of 11.5 GiB visible to WSL.
- The two sets ran back to back in one command (5 min 13 s).
- The build `PATH` in `campaign.txt` shows the campaign was launched from a
  VS Code server terminal, so editor processes were running. Their cost is
  not measurable.
- **Not observable or not controlled:** what else ran on the Windows host,
  the Windows power plan and mains power, CPU frequency scaling and thermal
  state, and WSL's own background activity. Treat the results as specific
  to this machine in this state, and compare only with result sets from it.

## 12. Anomalies and limitations

- No semantic failures; no failed, retried, or discarded runs.
- No `sec/op` comparison is significant after Holm correction; seven are
  below 0.05 before correction, all with B slower (sections 5 and 9).
- The criterion is met with little margin, and "no significant difference
  detected" is not equivalence (D-007, "Limitation").
- Session drift of roughly 5–10% on the large workloads, starting in set A,
  is confounded with set order.
- Microsecond-scale workloads (B1, B4, B5/n=3, B6) are noisy (± up to 35%);
  process start-up and scheduling dominate at that scale.
- No profiles were collected; the 2026-10-06 bottleneck analysis is not
  re-validated by this campaign, though the work per workload (allocations)
  is unchanged.

**Implication for Phase 4.** On this machine, a difference of a few percent
between two sequential sessions, or two sequential sets, cannot be told
apart from drift of the kind seen here. Before/after comparisons should
interleave the two builds in one session where practical (for example,
alternating per round), and any claimed speedup must be shown with
`benchstat` and with every run's semantic check passing.
